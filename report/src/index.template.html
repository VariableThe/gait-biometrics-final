Minor Specialisation Project · review of measured results

Cross-day verification went from one user refused 8 of 10 times to zero rejections

An evidence-first review of the gait-biometrics pipeline. Overnight, a multisensor descriptor plus lagged-covariance backend cut the frozen cross-day error from 7.0% EER with a 22% genuine-reject rate to 0.0% EER with no rejections — but that headline is exploratory reuse of five already-inspected users and the false-accept rate still sits at 1.5%, above target. A separate pooled-calibration study also improved the development false-accept rate. Every number is reproduced from committed report artefacts, negative results are kept visible, and reused test cohorts are labelled as such.

02 · Model development

Selection discipline: validation chooses, test only reports

Six neural methods were trained for 40 epochs at three seeds each, with three descriptor baselines, giving 15 validation candidates per dataset. The winner was chosen on validation EER, its artefacts were hashed and locked, and the test split was read once. The curves below show how much of the error was visible before the test split was touched — and how much of it appeared afterwards.

Dashed lines are the locked test EER of the same method. The gap between the two is the generalisation gap the study exists to expose.

What the protocol guarantees

  • Selection on validation only. No seed, threshold or post-processing choice was made from test scores; the seed ensemble is uniform, not best-of-three.
  • Locking with hashes. selection_lock.json freezes artefact hashes, development tensor hashes and the baseline checksum; the runner revalidates checkpoint config, seed, 40 completed epochs and protocol before reuse.
  • Reduced smoke runs cannot unlock a test evaluation.

What it cost

  • 36 final neural runs completed (6 methods × 3 seeds × 2 datasets), plus 15 descriptor candidates and an earlier validation-only sweep kept for provenance.
  • Summed per-run training time: 287.19 s (HAR) and 225.19 s (WISDM) on an RTX 5070 Ti, both sweeps running concurrently.
  • GPU training is not promised bitwise reproducible; the real TF32 check observed zero embedding difference with cuDNN TF32 disabled, and a preliminary TF32-enabled run exceeded tolerance, so that path is rejected rather than silently accepted.

The negative result, kept in view

HAR hybrid won validation at 3.04% EER and reached 21.46% on test — worse than the 17.64% baseline it replaced.

  • Per-seed test EER 0.2130 / 0.2004 / 0.2288, ensemble 0.2146.
  • The same pattern on WISDM: validation 1.98% → test 9.58%.
  • It is reported in full in the repository, and no later candidate was chosen from test scores to replace it.

04 · Cross-day & multisensor

The frozen cross-day error fell to zero — and the hard user was fixed

Santos v7 is controlled leg-mounted walking collected on two different days. The overnight hillclimb changed the representation (accelerometer + gyroscope + rotation-invariant descriptors), the backend (lagged covariance), and the enrollment scorer (a mean/two-template blend). Each change was selected on development folds, and its threshold was calibrated on five separate CALIB users before test access. The result below is the first time the pipeline separates all five test users across days — with an important caveat: the test cohort is the same five people already inspected in the earlier study, so this is exploratory, not a fresh blind evaluation.

Backend: lagged covariance wins

Lagged covariance (362-dimensional descriptor) won at 4.874% versus 5.885% for the multisensor control and 8.897% for the historical accel baseline under the same twenty splits.

What this study does establish. Cross-day verification is measurable and improvable under a frozen protocol: a change to representation and backend moved the same five test users from 7.0% to 0.0% EER and from 11 to 0 genuine rejections, without re-tuning the threshold on test data. Selection happened on development folds and separate calibration users.

Why it is not yet a deployment claim. The test cohort is the same five people seen before, so the improvement is partly a confirmation that the new features separate an already-observed population — not proof of generalisation to unseen people. The false-accept rate is still above target. Fresh repeated-session phone data remain the blocker.

05 · Calibration

A pooled threshold and a twelve-person refit lower the development false-accept rate

Separate from the hillclimb, this DEV-only study asks whether the operating point can be chosen more reliably. Twenty outer validation triples come from four balanced five-fold partitions of fifteen development people; each split holds out three people for calibration and three for validation, and fits the metric on the remaining nine (or twelve, for the refit). The promotion gate was fixed before fitting: blend FAR ≤ 1%, no increase in splits above 2% FAR, strictly lower mean FRR, and at least ten people no worse.

The concentrated-false-accept diagnostic triggered. Eight of the prior blend's eleven development false accepts sit in two directed person pairs. This flags claimant normalization as the next bounded research question; no normalization arm was added here, and those repeated decisions are not eleven independent people.

Per-person limits remain. Five people improve, seven tie, three worsen under C. Identity 9 is still completely rejected (100% mean FRR) and identity 5 stays at 67.5%. Passing the aggregate gate does not establish usable authentication for everyone.

What it establishes. A pooled threshold plus a twelve-person refit passes a fixed promotion gate on development data: lower mean FAR and FRR with no increase in bad-split count. Exact rational arithmetic is used throughout, and no original CALIB or TEST numerical payload was opened — this study neither changes the earlier frozen threshold nor establishes a better TEST FAR.

What it does not. These are descriptive results from overlapping development splits, not new held-out or population estimates. The order-statistic reference policy (D) assumes independent calibration observations, which does not hold for dependent pooled gait scores. Two hundred historical CPU covariance fits were run in total after a discarded first attempt.

06 · Subject generalisation

Bigger encoders did not help; simpler features did

Forty-eight complete runs tested a matched size × objective factorial on subject folds rather than random windows: width 32 vs 96, linear vs ArcFace identity heads, three subject folds × two seeds × exactly 40 epochs per neural run, with the source test split never loaded. Larger encoders helped WISDM slightly and made HAR slightly worse, and both lost to descriptor baselines.

Why this matters for the next step

  • Descriptor methods beat larger neural encoders on both sources when the problem is framed as subject generalisation rather than window discrimination.
  • The same descriptor family (wccn_auto) is what won the locked cross-day study — the two independent lines of evidence point the same way.

07 · Cost

What it costs to run

Data loading was rewritten for throughput, inference latency was measured on CPU and GPU, a compression experiment was run on the activity model, and the multisensor cross-day winner was timed against its baseline. Two of these results are clean wins; the compression experiment is a documented failure, and the accuracy gain has a measured latency cost.

Inference latency vs model size

Resident input, FP32, five warm-up batches, 30 timed repetitions. Excludes input transfer, data loading and scoring.

Fewer parameters, more time. The new ensemble stores 86,916 parameters against the baseline's 174,720 (the old count includes an unused DAE decoder) but is slower, because many small multiscale operations and three forwards add dispatch cost. Optional branch packing cuts CUDA batch-1 latency from 4.693 ms to 3.687 ms (1.27×) while growing the stored ensemble to 126,084 parameters and slowing CPU batch-128 from 76.5 ms to 92.8 ms. No blanket speed-up, phone-energy or on-device claim is made.

Compression experiment — a documented failure

Pruning the activity model bought size and cost accuracy. The accuracy loss was far too large to ship, so the branch is reported, not adopted.

Test activity accuracy fell from 95.31% to 66.41% (−30.3%) while parameters halved and FLOPs dropped by half. The size win is real; the accuracy loss is not acceptable, so the compressed model is not part of the pipeline.

Multisensor winner: an accuracy/latency trade-off

Resident raw-window-to-embedding CPU latency, batch one, thirty timed repetitions on two CPU threads. The cross-day winner is measurably slower than the acceleration baseline it replaces.

5.2× slower at batch one, not a speed-up. The multisensor winner needs 5.73 ms per window against the accel baseline's 1.11 ms (batch-32: 13.79 ms vs 4.27 ms). This is the measured cost of the accuracy gain, not an execution optimisation. Feature extraction, fixed projection and normalization are timed; sensor acquisition, file loading, enrollment/claim scoring and phone energy are excluded.

08 · Interpretation

What this project shows, and what it does not

The honest summary of a project at this stage: the pipeline works, the protocol is disciplined, cross-day verification improved dramatically overnight, and the deployment claims are still not earned — the biggest gains reuse a small already-inspected cohort.

Supported by the evidence

  • WISDM walking verification error more than halved (20.10% → 9.58%) under a validation-locked, test-read-once protocol, with ROC AUC 0.9622.
  • Cross-day verification on the same five test users moved from 7.0% EER / 22% FRR to 0.0% EER / 0% FRR with a multisensor + lagcov + blend pipeline — the formerly-refused user now passes all ten claims.
  • A pooled-threshold calibration policy (C) lowered development mean FAR from 0.917% to 0.836% and mean FRR from 19.28% to 15.13% under a fixed promotion gate.
  • Subject-fold studies consistently favour descriptor methods over larger neural encoders, and rotation-invariant descriptors transfer to both HAR and WISDM.
  • Data loading is 15.72× faster with bit-identical output; training and inference scale to a single consumer GPU.

Not yet established

  • Phone-based or wearable-based authentication of any kind.
  • Continuous passive authentication, online takeover detection or a usable false-accept operating point (the best cross-day FAR is still 1.5%, above the 1% target).
  • Generalisation beyond the five cross-day test users — the 0.0% EER result reuses the same people inspected earlier, so it is not a blind evaluation.
  • A fresh held-out cohort for the exploratory HAR descriptor result (10.44% EER), which reused a test cohort that had already been inspected.
  • Any on-device latency or energy claim; the multisensor winner is 5.2× slower than baseline at batch one.

Known weaknesses to fix first

  • The frozen false-accept rate still exceeds target. Six of seven lagcov false accepts target identity 26, and no threshold was re-chosen from the test gap.
  • Calibration per-person failures persist. Identity 9 is still 100% rejected and identity 5 stays near 67.5% under policy C — the aggregate gate hides unusable individuals.
  • The HAR neural winner generalised worse than the baseline it replaced, and the ensemble's advantage is unproven on that dataset.
  • Random-pair EER is not an authentication protocol; enrollment and session behaviour are only approximated.
  • Legacy numeric WISDM CSV labels remain unnamed and unverified; only the explicit wisdm_v1 mapping to the official AR v1.1 release is verified.
  • Overlapping windows are not independent, and pair draws use replacement — variance estimates are descriptive only.

Recommended next steps

Ordered by what unblocks the most, not by what is most interesting.

Confirm the cross-day win on a fresh cohort

The 0.0% EER reuses five already-inspected users. Recruit a new repeated-session phone cohort to turn the exploratory result into a blind one — this is the single highest-value next step.

Study claimant normalization

Eight of eleven development false accepts concentrate in two directed person pairs, and six of seven lagcov test accepts target one user. A normalization arm is the next bounded research question.

Fix the calibration per-user floor

Identity 9 is still 100% rejected and identity 5 stays near 67.5% under policy C. Until a per-user floor exists, continuous authentication is not deployable for everyone.

Reproduce the merged pipeline locally

Pull the merged branches and re-run the full pipeline end to end, so the WISDM identity handling and the cross-day numbers are independently reproduced rather than inherited from pull requests.

Regenerate processed WISDM with subject IDs

Re-export WISDM with subject identifiers attached, verify the old numeric labels' semantics, and record session boundaries instead of inferring identity from row order.

Decide the target device

Phone, wrist-worn or ankle-worn changes the windowing, sensor noise and energy budget — and the winner is already 5.2× slower. Optimising latency before that decision is wasted effort.

Methodology notes and protocol details

Verification protocol. Held-out-subject, within-recording discrimination. Development and test subjects are disjoint; validation windows never fit normalisation or the identity classifier. Scores are cosine similarity minus one, higher is more genuine, with 10,000 balanced same-activity comparisons drawn subject-uniform with replacement (5,000 genuine / 5,000 impostor).

Thresholds. The deployment threshold is selected on validation to target FAR ≤ 1% and then frozen; the test split reports FAR and FRR at that frozen threshold, which does not promise a 1% test FAR. EER is computed independently, and the nearest empirical crossing is labelled as a non-deployable evaluation point.

Cross-day protocol. Santos v7, controlled leg-mounted short walks over two days. Participant split (15 development / 5 calibration / 5 test) was fixed from directory metadata before signal inspection. Enrollment uses day 1 only, probing uses day 2 only. Coverage reflects supplied comparisons; exhaustive coverage is not verified.

Activity protocol. Six classes on UCI HAR (walking, upstairs, downstairs, sitting, standing, laying). The current sweep selects the checkpoint on validation activity loss; the historical numbers selected on training loss. 2,947 test windows.

Reproducibility caveats. Single-seed where noted; CUDA bitwise determinism is not guaranteed; validation was used for both checkpoint selection and threshold calibration; pair draws use replacement and overlapping raw windows are not independent; descriptive counts only, with no confidence intervals.
